# Writing rules — slide copy & speaker notes

## Action titles (the most important rule)
Every content slide title is a complete sentence stating the "so what". Topic labels are banned.

| Topic label | Action title |
|---|---|
| Results | Treatment effect holds across all three cohorts |
| Literature review | Prior work leaves the causal mechanism unexplained |
| Methodology | A sharp funding threshold lets us estimate the causal effect |
| Discussion | The effect survives controls for selection and attrition |

**Ghost-deck test** (also the engineer's): titles alone, read in order, tell the whole argument.

## Bullets
- ≤3 per slide, one idea each, phrases not sentences. >5 words/line and >3 lines is a warning (5/5/5).
- Telegraphic: drop articles/filler where meaning survives. "Our study found the intervention reduced
  costs significantly" → "Intervention cut costs 23% (p<.01)".
- Bullets orient; they don't transfer information — the presenter does that. If the audience is reading
  a paragraph, they're not listening (Mayer: redundancy hurts).

## Speaker notes
- 2–4 natural sentences: what you'd actually say, the *why*, the transition to the next slide.
- Not a re-read of the bullets. Include oral attribution before a citation appears.

## Voice — write for a person in a room (kill these AI tells)
- **Words to delete:** leverage, utilize, drive synergies, unlock potential, streamline, seamless,
  robust, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, end-to-end, "in today's rapidly evolving
  landscape", "at the intersection of", "not just X but Y", "imagine a world where".
- **Structural tells:** every bullet in identical parallel grammar; a perfectly balanced three points
  on every slide; titles that read like white-paper section headers; every slide the same rhythm.
- **Framing tells:** every problem relabeled an "opportunity"; three "benefits" that are one benefit
  said thrice; "we're uniquely positioned" with nothing naming the position.
- **Acid test:** would a specific human say this out loud to someone they respect? If no, rewrite.
- Vary sentence length. Short punches; longer carries nuance. Earn every abstraction with concrete
  evidence right before it. One genuine moment of personality every 5–7 slides.

## Numbers & specifics beat adjectives
"Strong traction" is invisible. "Box signed in March, Salesforce in pilot" lands. Name the customer,
the city, the number, the week. Specificity is credibility.

## Citations (academic integrity applies to slides)
- Every borrowed claim/figure/dataset is backed by the slide's `citations[]`; the builders print a small
  numbered footer and generate the **Sources & credits** slides. Details: audience-voice-and-citations.md §3–4.
- A figure from a paper gets a source caption beneath it; the caption must agree with the citation.

## Language & RTL
- Write in the deck's language. For Hebrew/Arabic: right-aligned; Latin tool/command tokens stay inline
  LTR; keep each run tagged with its language so the renderer picks a script-correct font. Units,
  numbers, code and mixed terms: audience-voice-and-citations.md §5.

## Closing copy
End on the conclusions/ask slide — the one action/belief to leave with. Never "Thank You" or a blank.
The conclusions slide stays on screen during Q&A, so make it the thing you want lingering.
